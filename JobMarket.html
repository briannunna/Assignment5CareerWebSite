Overall employment of physicists and astronomers is projected to grow 7 percent from 2025 to 2035, much faster than the average for all occupations. About 1,500 openings for physicists and astronomers are projected each year, on average, over the decade. Many of those openings are expected to result from the need to replace workers who transfer to different occupations or exit the labor force, such as to retire.

The median annual wage for astronomers was $128,820 in May 2025.
The median annual wage for physicists was $172,250 in May 2025.

Early-career postdoctoral researchers typically earn between $55,000 and $75,000, while established university professors and senior government scientists often see salaries ranging from $110,000 to over $180,000. Those who pivot into private industry—working as data scientists, software architects, or quantitative analysts—frequently command the highest compensation packages, sometimes exceeding $200,000 early in their corporate careers when factoring in bonuses and equity.
